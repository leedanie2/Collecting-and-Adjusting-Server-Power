# Poster results and data sources

Companion to the [poster PDF](../../Deliverables/Poster/APS-poster-Lee-48x36-v3-mlstyle_proof.pdf)
and [editable PPTX](../../Deliverables/Poster/APS-poster-Lee-48x36-v3-mlstyle.pptx).
This guide describes the October 2026 corrected analysis used by the poster.
[PAPER_DATA.md](PAPER_DATA.md) records the earlier paper's original analysis.

## Experiment and model

Three workloads (`hpl`, `aisim2`, `step`) × four conditions (`baseline`, `rampc`,
`powersmoother`, `usagegov`) × four repeat collections = 48 measured traces in
`data/runs/run{1,2,3,4}/`. The host is a dual Xeon Gold 6338, quiesced during
collection. The AI-style workload is a controlled workload, not a measurement
of a production training cluster.

The model copies one CPU-power trace to 10,000 synchronized servers, adds 300 W
of non-CPU power per server, applies load-dependent PUE (1.60 to 1.15), and
filters facility power through a 15 s UPS low-pass. The grid has a 50 MW base,
H = 2.5 s inertia, nominal 60 Hz, and a 25 kV connection with 100 MVA short-circuit
level and X/R = 7. These assumptions define the scenario; the study thresholds
(0.95 pu, 59.3 Hz, and 20 MW/min) are evaluation references, not a claim that
crossing a threshold demonstrates physical damage or establishes compliance.
See [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md) for the original reference collection.

`analysis/rescore_warm.py` reproduces the facility, swing-equation, and voltage
calculations in Python. It replaces the original fixed operating point with
each trace's mean, starts frequency at equilibrium, and discards the first 30 s.
Ramp & fill is trimmed to its workload plateau before grid scoring. Grid
metrics use a uniform 0.1 s grid (frequency uses a 0.01 s grid).

## R1 / R3: smoothing benefit and cost

| Method | CV reduction (%) | Extra runtime (%) | Extra CPU energy (%) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Ramp & fill | 91.4 ± 1.4 | 121.1 ± 5.7 | 161.0 ± 6.7 |
| Power smoother | 8.2 ± 3.5 | 3.5 ± 0.2 | 7.4 ± 0.6 |
| Governor | 21.2 ± 12.7 | 1.1 ± 1.9 | 11.6 ± 1.5 |

Each comparison uses its own workload/run baseline. Average the three workload
percent changes within each run, then report mean ± sample SD (`ddof=1`) across
four runs. Error bars are run-to-run variability, not confidence intervals.

- **CV:** `data/summary/rescored/scoreboard.csv`, rows with `warmup_s=30` and
  `workload=mean`. The stored `cv_pct_mean` is signed change; the poster reports
  its negative as a positive reduction. `rescored/matrix.csv` holds the 36
  comparisons per warm-up setting.
- **Cost:** `data/summary/scoreboard.csv`, `runtime_pct` and `energy_pct`, with
  their `_sd` columns. These are full, untrimmed measured CPU traces, so ramp
  flanks count toward the cost. They are unchanged by grid rescoring.
- **R1 trace illustration:** `data/runs/run1/hpl_*.csv`; the poster displays
  30–200 s and aligns ramp & fill to the start of its detected plateau. The
  illustrated peak-to-trough power swing is distinct from the CV metric.

The 45 s warm-up sensitivity rows give CV reductions of 91.4 ± 1.4%,
8.1 ± 3.5%, and 28.2 ± 12.1%, respectively. The governor result is sensitive to
both run and scoring window.

## R2: voltage swings

| Condition | Peak-to-trough voltage (% of nominal) |
|---|---:|
| Baseline | 1.411 ± 0.026 |
| Ramp & fill | 0.344 ± 0.005 |
| Power smoother | 1.366 ± 0.026 |
| Governor | 1.193 ± 0.047 |

Source: `data/summary/rescored/voltage_swing.csv`, generated by
`analysis/rescore_warm.py`. For each trace, calculate
`100 * (max(V_pu) - min(V_pu))` after the 30 s warm-up on the uniform 0.1 s grid.
Average the three workloads within each run, then take mean and sample SD over
four runs. The CSV also reports the mean of the per-trace minimum voltages,
aggregated in that same order. Those means differ from the worst-case minimum
in the fleet-size sweep below.

The poster rounds the swings to 1.41%, 0.34%, 1.37%, and 1.19%. Its reductions
of 76%, 3%, and 15% compare the aggregated swing with the aggregated baseline;
they are separate from the CV reductions of 91%, 8%, and 21%.

## Fleet-size context

Source: `data/summary/rescored/sweep.csv`. Each row reports the worst baseline
minimum voltage and frequency nadir across three workloads and four runs;
the two extremes need not come from the same trace.

| Servers | Minimum voltage (pu) | Frequency nadir (Hz) |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1,000 | 0.9954 | 59.983 |

| 10,000 | 0.9488 | 59.827 |

In this sweep, voltage crosses the study's 0.95 pu threshold before frequency
crosses its 59.3 Hz reference. The original model's 56.56 Hz result at 20,000
servers reflects its stale operating point and is superseded by this analysis.

## M1 / R4: telemetry and detection

The poster reports 10 Hz RAPL, 1 Hz `/proc`, 69 Redfish fields every 10 s, PDU
power at approximately 1.8 Hz, and a Grafana refresh of 5 s. Sampling costs
2.19% of one core (0.017% of the 128-thread host) and +0.15 W for the daemon.
These are reported measurement results; they are not outputs of the grid replica.
The database and other monitoring services have additional overhead, which is
why the experiment quiesces the host. See [measurement](../measurement/README.md).

The poster reports OLS detecting 19 of 1,735 spikes, random forest plus Kalman
PR-AUC 0.68 with 10–20 s late flags, and a run-queue precursor with Cohen's
d = 1.5 and Hawkes branching 0.84–0.96. These exploratory results did not yield
reliable advance warning. They depend on telemetry caches omitted from the repo;
see [prediction](../prediction/README.md) for code and cache regeneration.

The final utilization detector responds in about one 10 Hz sample, with 5
alarms/hour in the poster's detector comparison. Independently, the governor's
actuation evaluation recalls 8/24 transitions (33%), at 19% precision and
median lead −0.23 s. It uses the separate `data/detector/` collection, not the
48-trace matrix, and phase anchors have ±0.5 s uncertainty. Regenerate it with
`analysis/score_detector.py`; see [collection details](data/detector/README.md).

## Reproduce

From the repository root:

```bash
python3 -m pip install -r Code/grid-simulation/requirements.txt
cd Code/grid-simulation
python3 analysis/rescore_warm.py --selfcheck
python3 analysis/rescore_warm.py
python3 analysis/summarize_n4.py
python3 analysis/score_detector.py
```

The replica selfcheck compares all 12 original reference cells with checked-in
Simulink outputs: CV ratio within 6%, frequency RMS error below 2 mHz, and
maximum voltage error below 0.001 pu. Regeneration writes corrected grid tables
under `data/summary/rescored/`; the cost command writes the original scoreboard
under `data/summary/`, whose grid columns describe the earlier model.
